Concrete Lifting
& Leveling

Sunken driveways, uneven walkways, pool decks with a lip at every joint. We raise the slab you already have back to grade by filling the void underneath it — no demolition, no replacement, no waiting for concrete to cure.

The method

Fill the void,
raise the slab

Concrete does not usually sink because there is something wrong with the concrete. It sinks because the soil beneath it went somewhere — washed out by poor drainage, consolidated under load, or lost into a void that opened up below.

We drill a series of ports about the diameter of a coin through the slab and inject high-density structural polyurethane. The foam expands into the empty space beneath, fills it completely, and then, as it keeps expanding against the compacted soil, lifts the slab back to grade. We control the rate with the injection itself and watch the elevation the entire time.

It cures in minutes. We patch the ports, and you can drive on it the same day. Compared with tearing the slab out and pouring new concrete, it is typically a fraction of the cost and finishes in hours rather than weeks.

One detail that matters on our soils in particular: the foam weighs a small fraction of what a traditional mud slurry weighs. On weak or washed-out ground, adding several tons of wet slurry to soil that already failed is a way to be back in a year. Foam does not do that.

What we lift

If it is flat
and it has dropped

Anything poured on grade is a candidate, as long as the slab itself is sound. If it has broken into small pieces, replacement is the honest answer and we will say so.

01

Driveways and aprons

The apron at the garage and the section nearest the street settle first. A lip there catches bumpers and channels water back toward the house.

02

Walkways and steps

Uneven sidewalk joints are the most common trip hazard on a residential property, and a genuine liability if you have visitors or tenants.

03

Pool decks

Very common on the coast. Water finds its way under the deck, carries sand out, and the slab drops away from the coping in a ring around the pool.

04

Garage and interior floors

Sunken garage slabs, shop floors and AC pads. Interior work is possible too, where a slab has settled over a void.

Doing it once

Lifting the slab is
the easy part

A slab that dropped once will drop again if nothing changes underneath it. Lifting without addressing the water that caused it is a temporary result at a permanent price.

Included with the lift
  • Locating and filling the void completely, not just enough to raise it
  • Elevation monitored throughout so the lift is controlled
  • Ports patched, and drilling kept to the minimum needed
  • Joints and cracks sealed so surface water stops running underneath
  • The slab back in service the same day, vehicles included
Fixing the cause
  • Finding where the water is coming from — gutter, grading or plumbing
  • Downspout discharge moved clear of the slab
  • Regrading so runoff is directed away rather than under
  • Soil stabilization injection where the ground itself is weak
  • An honest recommendation to replace where lifting will not hold
FAQs

Questions we
get asked

How much cheaper is lifting than replacing?

Lifting is normally a substantial saving against removal and replacement, and the difference grows on larger areas and on hard-to-access slabs. The exact figure depends on the size of the slab, how far it has to come up and how much void there is to fill, which is why we measure before quoting.

There is a second saving people forget: no demolition means no damaged landscaping, no heavy equipment across your lawn, and no dumpster in the driveway for a week.

How long before I can use it?

The foam reaches most of its strength within about fifteen minutes. Walkways are usable immediately, and driveways will take vehicle traffic the same day, usually within the hour. There is no cure period like new concrete, which needs days before it takes a car.

Will the slab drop again?

Not because of the foam, which does not wash out, compress or break down. It can drop again if whatever removed the original soil is still active, which is why we spend time finding the water source and correcting it as part of the job.

If we look at your slab and conclude that lifting will not hold, we will tell you that rather than take the work.

Can you lift a cracked slab?

Often, yes. A slab with a few cracks can usually be lifted and the cracks sealed afterward. What we cannot do much with is a slab that has already broken into many small pieces or has badly spalled, deteriorated concrete — at that point there is nothing structurally continuous left to lift, and replacement is the right call.

Do you do commercial and HOA work?

Yes. Sidewalk trip hazards, parking areas, warehouse floors, loading docks and condominium and pool decks are all common jobs for us. Because the work is fast and does not require closing an area for days, it suits properties that cannot afford long shutdowns.
