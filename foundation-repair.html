The short version

Foundations do not
fail. Soil does.

Concrete and block are extremely strong in compression. What they cannot do is span a gap. When the soil under one part of a foundation moves and the soil under the rest of it does not, the structure has to bridge the difference — and it cracks where it cannot.

That is differential settlement, and it is behind most of the foundation damage we see in Escambia and Santa Rosa County. Around here the soil moves for reasons that are very specific to the Gulf Coast:

  • Sandy soils that erode. Our sand drains beautifully — and travels. Concentrated runoff from a roof valley or a bad downspout can wash material out from under a footing in a few seasons.
  • Uncompacted fill. Sites built up with fill that was never properly compacted keep consolidating under the weight of the house for years after the slab is poured.
  • A high water table. Much of the area sits close to groundwater. Heavy tropical rain raises it further, softening bearing soil and pushing on below-grade walls.
  • Organic and clay pockets. Buried organics compress as they break down; clay lenses shrink in drought and swell when the rain returns, heaving what is above them.
  • Plumbing leaks. A slow leak under a slab saturates and erodes soil continuously, in one spot, for as long as it goes unnoticed.

Every one of those has a different fix. Which is why the inspection matters more than the sales pitch.

What we repair

Problems we solve
every week

If you are looking at one of these, it has a known cause and a known fix. The variable is how far it has progressed.

01

Foundation settlement

One area of the house drops relative to the rest. Piers carry that load down to soil that can hold it, stopping the movement and often recovering elevation.

02

Bowing & leaning walls

Saturated soil pushes laterally on block and poured walls until they bow inward. Wall braces and anchors take that load and can straighten the wall over time.

03

Floor & slab cracks

Cracks that keep opening, or spread across a room, mean the slab is spanning something. We support it, fill the void beneath, and seal the crack.

04

Uneven & sagging floors

Above a crawlspace this is usually failing piers or a sagging girder. We reset the support system on proper footings and bring the floor back toward level.

05

Tilting chimneys & stoops

Chimneys and porches are often on shallower footings than the house, so they move first — and pull away visibly. Piering brings them back and holds them.

06

Foundation cracks

Not all cracks are structural. We tell you which yours is, then either repair and monitor it, or address the movement underneath it first.

07

Soil & drainage failures

Water is the usual culprit. Correcting grading, downspout discharge and subsurface drainage protects the repair and keeps it from recurring.

08

Garage & lintel movement

Wide garage openings concentrate load on a small bearing area. Movement shows up over the door first, and is straightforward to correct early.

Repair methods

The systems
we install

We match the method to the failure and to your soil. No single product solves every foundation problem, and any contractor who says otherwise is selling from a truck inventory rather than from an inspection.

Method 01

Helical Piers

Steel shafts with helical bearing plates, screwed into the ground until they reach load-bearing strata. Installation torque is measured continuously, which gives a direct read on the capacity of every individual pier. Ideal for lighter structures, additions, new construction and wet sites where excavation is impractical.

Helical piers in detail
Method 02

Push Piers

Steel pier sections driven to depth using the weight of the structure itself as the reaction force. Because the house does the pushing, each pier is effectively load-tested as it is installed. Well suited to heavier masonry homes with real settlement to recover.

Method 03

Wall Braces & Anchors

For walls being pushed inward by lateral soil pressure. Steel braces resist further movement immediately; where there is room outside, anchors allow the wall to be tightened back toward plumb over a series of adjustments rather than all at once.

Method 04

Crawlspace Support Posts

Adjustable galvanized steel posts on engineered footings, replacing stacked block, rotted wood shims and undersized jacks. They take the bounce out of a floor and let it be brought back toward level in controlled increments.

Floor supports in detail Method 05

Polyurethane Injection

High-density structural foam injected through dime-sized ports to fill voids beneath a slab and raise it back to grade. Cures in minutes, adds almost no weight to already weak soil, and leaves the surface intact.

Concrete lifting in detail
Method 06

Crack Repair & Sealing

Once movement is arrested, cracks get repaired properly — epoxy or polyurethane injection for poured concrete, engineered carbon fiber or steel reinforcement where a wall needs added tensile strength. Never as a substitute for fixing the cause.

Our process

What a repair
actually looks like

01

Inspect & measure

Interior, exterior and underneath. We take elevation readings across the floor to map the movement, document cracks, and check grading, gutters and drainage around the whole perimeter.

02

Diagnose the cause

We work out what moved and why — erosion, fill consolidation, water intrusion, a plumbing leak. The cause drives the design; the symptoms only tell us where to look.

03

Design & price the fix

You receive a written plan: pier type, pier locations and count, depths we expect, what will be restored, what will not, and a fixed price. Engineered drawings where the job calls for them.

04

Install & document

Our crews install to spec and log installation torque or drive pressure at every pier. You get those records at closeout, along with the warranty. They matter at resale.

Foundation repair FAQs

Questions we
get asked

What happens if I do not fix a foundation crack?

If the crack is the result of active movement, it keeps opening. The damage then works outward from the foundation into everything attached to it: door and window frames rack out of square, drywall and brick separate, tile fails along the same lines, and rigid supply and drain lines come under stress they were never designed for.

None of that is sudden, which is exactly why it gets ignored. The repair scope grows quietly, year over year, until the fix is several times what it would have been.

How do I know whether a crack is structural?

A few reliable signals: cracks wider than about a quarter inch, cracks that are wider at one end than the other, diagonal or stair-step cracks in block and brick, horizontal cracks in a foundation wall, and any crack that is visibly larger than it was six months ago. Horizontal cracking is the one to treat most urgently, because it indicates lateral pressure on the wall.

Fine vertical hairlines in a poured wall are frequently just curing shrinkage. We will tell you which kind you have, and we will say so even when the answer means we do not sell you anything.

Will my house be lifted back to level?

Often, at least partially. The first objective of any repair is to stop the movement; recovering elevation is the second. How much comes back depends on how long the settlement has been going on, what the structure is built from, and whether lifting would crack finishes that have since adjusted to the settled position.

We will give you a realistic expectation before the work starts. Anyone promising a perfectly level floor sight-unseen has not measured your house yet.

Does foundation repair come with a warranty?

Yes. Our structural repairs carry a written warranty, and the terms are handed over at closeout together with the installation records for every pier. Read the transferability clause on any warranty you are offered — a warranty that transfers to the next owner is worth considerably more when you sell.

Will homeowners insurance cover it?

Usually not. Most policies exclude settlement, soil movement and earth shifting, which is what causes the majority of foundation damage. Damage arising from a sudden covered event — a burst supply line under the slab, for example — is sometimes treated differently.

It is always worth calling your carrier and asking, and we are happy to provide documentation of what we find to support a claim.

How soon should I have it looked at?

Now, if you are noticing multiple symptoms at once, if anything changed after a heavy storm season, or if a crack has visibly grown. The inspection is free and carries no obligation, so the only thing waiting buys you is a larger repair.
