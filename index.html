Why homes move here

The problem is rarely
the crack you can see

A stair-step crack in the block, a door that will not latch, a floor that slopes — those are symptoms. The cause is almost always in the ground: poorly compacted fill from the original build, sandy soil washing out from under a footing, a high water table, or organic soil that keeps compressing under load.

Patching the crack without addressing the root cause is simply putting a Band-Aid on it. We inspect the whole structure — inside, outside, and underneath — take elevation readings, and identify the mechanism before we quote a repair. Then we fix the cause, and the symptoms stop coming back.

Warning signs

What your house is
trying to tell you

Any of these symptoms below are reasons to get a foundation inspection.

01

Sloping or bouncy floors

Floors that dip toward the center of the house, or feel springy underfoot, usually point to failing supports or rotted framing in the crawlspace.

02

Stair-step wall cracks

Diagonal cracking that follows the mortar joints in block or brick is a classic differential settlement signature.

03

Sticking doors & windows

When openings rack out of square, the frame around them has moved. Planing the door treats the symptom, not the cause.

04

Cracked slab or tile

Long cracks running through a slab, or tile that keeps popping loose along the same line, indicate the slab is no longer fully supported.

05

Musty smell indoors

Most of the air on your first floor comes up from the crawlspace. If it smells damp downstairs, you are breathing that upstairs.

06

Standing water underneath

Water pooling in the crawlspace after rain means drainage has failed. Sustained moisture leads to rot, mold and pest pressure.

07

Gaps at trim & ceilings

Separation where crown molding meets the ceiling, or baseboard that separates from the flooring, shows the structure is deflecting.

08

Standing water outside the home

Water pooling in the yard outside the home can lead to extensive foundation concerns. French drains could be needed.

Every structure type

Slab, crawlspace
or basement

We see all three, often on the same street. Each fails differently, and each needs a different repair. Here is what we look for under yours.

Slab on grade

Soil washes out or consolidates beneath the slab, leaving it unsupported; the slab then cracks where it spans the void. We stabilize with piers and fill voids from above.

Crawlspace

Common in older and coastal-elevated homes. Piers settle, girders sag and humidity rots the framing from below. We reset the supports, then seal the space so it stops working against you.

Basement

Less common here, and all the more important to get right. Saturated soil pushes walls inward and water finds every joint. We brace the wall, relieve the pressure and manage the water.

Questions

Straight
answers

Do not see yours? Ask us during the inspection — there is no charge and no obligation either way.

How much does foundation repair cost?

It depends on what is moving and how far it has gone. A handful of crawlspace support posts is a modest job; piering a settled corner of a slab home is a larger one. The honest answer is that nobody can quote it responsibly over the phone.

What we can promise is that the inspection is free, and that we will tell you if the problem is cosmetic and does not warrant structural work at all.

Is foundation repair worth doing?

Structural movement does not stabilize on its own, and the cost curve only goes one way. What starts as a hairline crack becomes racked door frames, then separated brick, then plumbing under stress.

It also shows up at closing. Documented, warrantied repair work from a licensed contractor is far easier to sell than an unexplained crack and a nervous buyer.

What causes foundation problems on the Gulf Coast?

Water and soil, in that order. Sandy soils here drain fast but also erode fast, so runoff can carry material out from under a footing. Poorly compacted fill under newer construction settles under load. Organic and clay pockets compress or shrink and swell with moisture. And a high water table keeps crawlspaces wet and softens bearing soil.

How long does the work take?

Most residential projects run one to three days on site. Larger piering jobs and full encapsulations can run longer. We give you a realistic schedule with the written plan, and if something changes, you hear it from us that day rather than at the end.

Do I have to move out during the repair?

Almost never. The great majority of our work happens outside the house or underneath it, and homeowners stay put throughout. If any part of a job would genuinely disrupt living in the home, we tell you before you sign, not after.
